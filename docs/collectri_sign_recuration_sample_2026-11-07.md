# Item 2 of `docs/exploration_queue.md`: a larger seeded recuration of disputed CollecTRI signs (7 November 2026)

Committed as a record on 7 November 2026. It is the per-pair detail behind the second sample recorded in the dated addition at the end of `docs/regulation_sign_recuration.md`. Nothing in the graph or the labels is changed by it; whether the pooled estimate is narrow enough to set a latent-sign prior is the owner's call (decision 9.72).

A research pass on Fable 5.1 (claude-fable-5-1), 7 November 2026, for the caller to file; nothing in the repository was
edited, committed or pushed. The six reading batches ran as sub-agents started with `model: fable`; each states in its
report that it ran on Fable throughout (one first attempt at batch 6 sent a request on claude-opus-5 and was discarded
unread and redone from nothing on Fable, under CLAUDE.md "Models"). Files beside this one in the same folder:
`item2_new_sample_verdicts.csv` (the 60 pairs), `pooled_verdicts.csv` (the 76 pairs, both samples),
`item2_papers_read.csv` (every paper record read, with its class and quote), `sample_seed20261107.csv` (the draw with
the PubMed ids each release cites), `regulation_edges_v5.csv` (the frame), `item2_collectri_recuration_references.bib`
(the BibTeX, also at the end of this file), `quote_check/results_*.csv` (the re-reading of every quote), and
`batch_K_papers.csv`, `batch_K_verdicts.csv`, `batch_K_bib.bib`, `batch_K_report.md` for K = 1 to 6.

## 1. The frame, rebuilt on v5, and the sample

**Graph.** `data/processed/graph_full_neuronal_split_binders_drugs_v5`, the build of 5 November 2026 (edges SHA-256
`aa1a9cb2…`, 275,006 edges, 47,630 `regulates_transcription_of`, evidence source CollecTRI), read with its
`edge_evidence.parquet` of the same build. The 17 October record was measured on the v5 build of that date (41,638
regulation edges, 3,795 disputed, 1,930 disputed labels); the graph has been rebuilt since (decision 9.76's columns are
in the table now), so the frame was rebuilt rather than reused, as the brief asked, and the counts below are of
today's build. A measurement carries the graph it was taken on.

**Frame.** The same definition as `docs/regulation_sign_recuration.md`: regulation labels (decision 9.63's
`curated_edge_labels` with its defaults, 16,812 regulation labels on this build) whose two CollecTRI releases in the
pinned evidences download (`data/raw/omnipath/collectri_interactions_evidences.json`, SHA-256 `60b79abc…`, unchanged)
state opposite signs: each release states exactly one sign and they differ (a release stating both is `sign_disputed`
but not in the frame, as before). Rebuilt by `build_frame.py` from `graph/sign_evidence.py`'s own functions
(`sign_reference_table`, `signs_stated_by`), one row per v5 edge in edge order. **Size: 2,166 labels** (2,255
`sign_disputed` labels less 89 where a release states both), in the two strata by the release the stored sign follows:
**1,688 follow CollecTRI2** (710 overruling a sign the 2023 release took from a paper, 850 a default activation, 128 a
regulon inference) and **478 follow the 2023 release** (167 PMID, 6 PMID+default, 281 default activation, 24 regulon).
On 17 October the strata were 1,502 and 428. All 16 pairs of the 17 October sample are in the frame with the same
stratum and sign decision as that record states (checked pair by pair), which is the evidence that the frame is the
same one.

**Sample.** The 16 pairs already read were removed (pool 2,150) and **30 labels were drawn from each stratum with
pandas' `sample(30, random_state=20261107)`** over the pool in edge order, the same construction as
`sample(8, random_state=20261017)` on 17 October: **60 pairs**, 30 per stratum (CollecTRI2 stratum: 13 PMID, 16
default, 1 regulon; 2023 stratum: 12 PMID, 15 default, 3 regulon). Sixty were drawn against the target of 40
decidable because the first sample had 5 of 16 undecidable; this sample had 22 of 60 undecidable, so **38 decidable
pairs** were added, two short of the target. No further draw was made: the shortfall is reported rather than topped
up, so that the sample stays the one the seed gives.

**What was read.** For each pair, the PubMed ids each release puts behind its own sign, split into the ids only one
release cites and the ids both cite, with the 17 October cap: all of them when a release's list held eight or fewer,
otherwise the first eight (here by ascending PMID; the 17 October record does not state its order) of the ids only
that release cites, plus every shared id. 295 cited records were listed; every one was read. The batches read 315
records in all: 295 cited (131 cited by both releases, 135 by CollecTRI2 only, 29 by the 2023 release only) and 20
found by the one PubMed search the rule allows per pair with no informative citation (plus Europe PMC as the second
source where that search returned nothing). Classes: 86 activates,
61 represses, 14 both,
68 indirect or not transcriptional,
84 not about this regulation, 2 no abstract
(PMIDs 9457682 and 19411829, "[Abstract not available]" in PubMed and no abstractText in Europe PMC). Two cited papers
are typed Retracted Publication by PubMed and Europe PMC (17369855 behind AR to PEG10, 19179414 behind NFKB1 to IL12B)
and were classed but not weighed.

**Decision rule.** The 17 October rule, verbatim in the brief given to every batch (`reading_brief.md`): class each
abstract as activates, represses, both, indirect or not transcriptional, not about this regulation, or no abstract,
with the supporting sentence copied verbatim; the pair's verdict is the direct transcriptional sign the cited papers
support (activation, repression, both reported, undetermined); decidable means activation or repression; one PubMed
search of up to five results where no cited paper is informative, marked found by search. Every quote was read
through the PubMed MCP tool (`get_article_metadata`) unless the row's `read_where` says Europe PMC (where PubMed's text
drops italicised gene names, the batch quoted the Europe PMC record). The E-utilities host is disallowed by its robots
file for `experiments/read_public_page.py` and was not read.

## 4. Is the interval narrow enough to set a latent-sign prior?

Neither `docs/regulation_sign_recuration.md` nor item 2 states a width or a standard: the record says only that
"Sixteen pairs is a sample, not an estimate with useful precision" and the item that 0.35 to 0.85 is "too wide to set
a latent-sign prior from". So the answer is given against what a prior would be used for. The posterior interval has
halved (0.27 wide) and now lies on one side of the question that matters: its upper end, 0.63, is below any value at
which the stored sign of a disputed label would be worth more than a weak hint, and it straddles 0.5, so **on a
disputed label the stored sign is indistinguishable from a coin flip** (24 right, 25 wrong). A latent-sign prior for
the 2,166 disputed labels would therefore be set at about 0.5 for the stored sign, or, if the prior is allowed to use
the release, about 0.6 for CollecTRI2's sign and 0.4 for the 2023 release's, with the tier making no difference
(0.50 for a paper-decided 2023 sign, 0.50 for a default). That is a usable prior in the sense that it is nearly flat
and its centre is known to about ±0.14; it is not a prior that would let the fit lean on the stored sign. Whether a
flat prior is what the owner wants to register, or whether the disputed labels should instead stay gated (decision
9.72 option (a)) or enter as two gated edges of opposite sign (option (c)), is the owner's decision; this pass's
reading is that the data now say the sign is unknown rather than that more sampling would sharpen it, since a
further 60 pairs would narrow the interval by about a third while the point estimate has moved from 0.64 to 0.49.
Note also that this is the proportion right on **disputed labels**, not on the 29,000 regulation labels the releases
agree on, which the sample says nothing about.
