## 2. Task 1 — localisation, and whether MAR05430's SLC9A9 catalysis is physiological

**NHE9 is an endosomal protein; it is not at the neuronal plasma membrane in any meaningful amount.** Every primary
measurement read puts it on endosomal membranes: late recycling endosomes in human cell lines (Q1), sorting and
recycling endosomes (Q6), and recycling endosomes in mouse cortical astrocytes with a measured Mander's coefficient of
0.46 against Rab11 and 0.11 against EEA1, and none against the late-endosome lipid marker (Q18). UniProt's "Cell
membrane" line for NHE9 is **not** a human measurement: its evidence code is `ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q8BZ00`, by
similarity to the mouse protein, and the accompanying note restricts it to one specialised cell type, the inner ear
hair bundle (Q4), where the functional rationale is the high K+ of the endolymph rather than anything in a neuron
(Q5). No source read reports a neuronal surface fraction for NHE9.

**NHE6 does reach the plasma membrane, but as a transient of endosomal recycling, not as a resident pool.** UniProt
states it is "Present predominantly in the recycling compartments … but undergoes plasma membrane localization during
vesicular recycling", with a constitutive plasmalemmal pool only in a few specialised cell types (vestibular hair
bundles, osteoblasts) and that by similarity (Q3). The second source is independent and experimental: RACK1 knockdown
raised recycling-endosome pH and lowered surface NHE6 without changing total NHE6, which the authors read as RACK1
"regulating the distribution of NHE6 between endosomes and the plasma membrane" (Q6). In neurons specifically, NHE6 is
punctate in soma and neurites with accumulation at spines and presynaptic terminals and partial colocalisation with
early- and recycling-endosome markers (Q7) — i.e. vesicular, in the compartment, at the synapse. One paper reports a
large plasma-membrane fraction of NHE6, and it is a **mutant**: the de novo G218R gain-of-function variant, where "a
major fraction was delivered to the plasma membrane and endocytic pathways" (Ilie et al. 2019, abstract) — that is a
statement about a disease allele's mistrafficking, not about wild-type NHE6, and it should not be cited for the
wild-type localisation.

**MAR05430's SLC9A9 catalysis is not physiological.** MAR05430 is "transport of H+ and Na+ (cytosol to
extracellular)", the plasma-membrane exchange SLC9A1–5 perform. Three things make the SLC9A9 placement an artefact
rather than a modelling choice:

1. the evidence Human-GEM itself carries for it is a **prediction**: `genes.tsv` annotates SLC9A9 as
   "Cell membrane;Lysosome" with the source column reading `DeepLoc2` (Q26), a sequence-based localisation predictor,
   not a measurement;
2. neither of the two predicted compartments matches the curated annotation. The "Cell membrane" half is the
   by-similarity hair-cell case (Q4); the "Lysosome" half is contradicted outright by the one systematic
   isoform-localisation study, "No significant localization of these NHEs was observed in lysosomes" (Q1), and by the
   absence of NHE9 from the late-endosome marker in astrocytes (Q18);
3. the formal reaction is the right antiport but on the wrong membrane. UniProt's catalytic activity for NHE9 is
   Rhea:29419, "Na(+)(in) + H(+)(out) = Na(+)(out) + H(+)(in)", with a K+ variant (Rhea:29467). Topologically the
   endosomal lumen occupies the same side as "out", so the *stoichiometry* MAR05430 carries is not wrong — only its
   compartments are. This is why the placement is easy to make and easy to miss: it moves the neuronal cytosolic and
   extracellular Na+ pools and extracellular H+ (as the brief states for v5), when the physiological effect is on an
   endosomal lumen the graph does not have.

A consequence worth recording: because Human-GEM holds SLC9A9 in exactly one gene rule (this pass confirmed
`G_ENSG00000181804` appears once in a `geneProductRef`, inside the MAR05430 block of
`data/raw/Human-GEM/model/Human-GEM.xml`), **a perturbation of SLC9A9 on v5 can only act through a non-physiological
edge.** SLC9A6 (`ENSG00000198689`) does not occur in `Human-GEM.xml` at all — zero matches, this pass's own check —
which matches the brief's "no node".

Every arrow below is the sign a graph edge would carry under **loss of function** of the named gene. The pump-and-leak
logic is the one already quoted in the item from the Kondapalli 2014 review, and the primary papers now supply the
sign for each isoform and each downstream step.

Two signs are worth stating carefully because they look paradoxical and a careless edge would get them backwards.
First, **more acid does not mean more degradation**: NHE6-null neurons are *more* acidic in both endosome and lysosome
and yet show *less* lysosomal proteolysis (Q12), because the failure is in trafficking and fusion, not in acidity.
Second, the effect on surface receptor levels runs through pH, not through the receptor: NHE9 overexpression
*alkalinises* and *raises* surface GLAST and transferrin receptor (Q17), so loss lowers them (Q19) — the pH is the
mediator and the surface transporter is the readout.

A redundancy caveat that matters for any edge: knockdown of NHE9 in *mouse* cortical astrocytes did **not** reach
significance for endosomal pH (Q19: "the difference fell short of significance"), and NHE6 and NHE9 colocalise
extensively in those cells, which the authors read as NHE6 compensating. The significant acidification was in *human*
glioma cells. So a graph edge "loss of NHE9 → endosomal pH down" is supported in human cells and is compensated in
mouse astrocytes; that species split belongs in the edge's note.

## 4. Task 3 — NHE9's disease association (now sourced)

The item said this was unsourced. It is sourced as follows.

**Autism.** The origin is Morrow et al. 2008 in *Science* (Q20): human, homozygosity mapping in consanguineous
families followed by resequencing in patients with unrelated parents; NHE9 is named among the genes with "additional
potential mutations in patients with unrelated parents". The specific findings, as Kondapalli et al. restate them
(Q21): a large deletion upstream of the 5' end of NHE9 but not in the coding region; a nonsense mutation giving a stop
at Arg423; and rare non-conservative coding variants more common in ASD-with-epilepsy than in controls, 5.95% against
0.63%. The functional follow-up is Kondapalli et al. 2013 (Q17): three autism-associated variants (L236S, S438P,
V176I) all scored as loss of function in mouse primary cortical astrocytes, two of them also in yeast complementation.

**ADHD.** Two human genetic studies of different design. Lasky-Su et al. 2008 (Q22): GWAS of six quantitative ADHD
phenotypes in 909 proband-parent trios, 429,981 autosomal SNPs, where SLC9A9 "had the strongest overall associations"
among a pre-specified 37-gene candidate list — and the authors themselves say replication is necessary. Markunas et
al. 2010 (Q23): family-based association on Conners' parent and teacher scales and the Continuous Performance Test,
where rs1046706 in the SLC9A9 3' UTR survived FDR correction for hyperactive-impulsive and total symptom scores and
for errors of commission. Zhang-James et al. 2019 (Q24) adds molecular, not genetic, evidence: the ADHD-associated
A409P alters the NHE9 interactome and the ASD nonsense R423X yields no detectable protein.

**Three limits that must not be overstated.**
- Neither ADHD study is a genome-wide significant hit for SLC9A9 in a diagnosis-based GWAS. Q22's own
  genome-wide-significant SNPs were in CDH13 and GFOD1, not SLC9A9; SLC9A9's standing there is as a candidate gene in
  a pre-specified list. A third study found no genome-wide significant result at all and its gene-based top hit was
  GPR139, not SLC9A9 (Ebejer et al. 2013, PMID 23527680, read as abstract on 7 November 2026) — so the ADHD
  association is **suggestive and unreplicated at genome-wide significance**, which is how the queue should carry it.
- The original autism deletion was *upstream* of NHE9 and not in the coding region (Q21). Morrow et al.'s own framing
  is about "defective regulation of gene expression after neural activity" (Q20), not about a coding loss.
- Kondapalli et al. flag the alternative reading themselves: "It is possible that the variants associated with autism
  or ADHD represent benign polymorphisms since additional changes in other genes were not investigated or ruled out"
  (full text, PMC3815575).

**A curated second source the repository already holds.** `data/raw/sfari/animal_genes.csv` (this pass's own local
check, 7 November 2026) gives, under the header
`status,gene-symbol,gene-name,human-gene-score,human-syndromic,syntenic,model-species,number-of-models,has-rescue-model`:
`9,SLC9A9,"solute carrier family 9 member A9",2,0,Yes,"Mus musculus",3,0` and
`9,SLC9A6,"solute carrier family 9, subfamily A",1,1,No,"Mus musculus",3,0`. So SFARI scores SLC9A6 1 and syndromic,
and SLC9A9 2 and not syndromic — a curated gene-level ranking that is consistent with the literature above, and one
the build can read without a new fetch.

## 5. Task 4 — the second-source rule, applied

The rule was applied claim by claim rather than paper by paper. The table in §3 names the second source for each sign;
the pattern is that the two arms with the strongest support are two-sourced across **independent laboratories**, and
the arms that are not are marked.

- **Endosomal overacidification on NHE6 loss**: Morrow lab (Q8, Q12, Q14) and, independently, the Orlowski/McKinney
  lab at McGill (Q16, and Q6's RACK1 pH result from the Kanazawa lab in Osaka for the wild-type leak). Three labs.
- **TrkB/BDNF**: Morrow lab (Q8) + Petkovic/Fuster, Basel and Bern (Q9) + Orlowski/McKinney (Q10) + the human iPSC
  study (Q11). Four, in three species contexts (mouse brain, mouse cochlea, human iPSC neurons).
- **Tau**: rat model (Q14, Morrow lab) + human post-mortem from an unrelated group (Q15, Garbern et al., Wayne State
  and Penn). Two, and crucially one of them human tissue.
- **Autism association of NHE9**: human genetics (Q20, Walsh lab) + functional (Q17, Rao lab) + a curated database
  (SFARI, §4) + a third molecular study (Q24).
- **ADHD association of NHE9**: Q22 (Faraone/Lasky-Su) + Q23 (Ashley-Koch, Duke) — two independent cohorts and
  designs — plus a **negative** third (Ebejer et al. 2013), which the rule's spirit requires reporting rather than
  omitting.
- **Single-sourced, and marked as such**: M6PR/retromer (Q12), exosome release up (Q13), amyloid-β (Q14), synaptic
  glutamate clearance down (the authors' extension in Q19).

No source failed to load in a way that needed a second source substituted. The one refusal is recorded in §7 and a

## 6. Task 5 — what Reactome and UniProt already describe, and whether an import could use it

**This is the pass's main finding, and it corrects the premise of the question.** The question asks about "a release
later than 98". There is no such release: `https://reactome.org/ContentService/data/database/version` returned **98**
on 7 November 2026, so release 98 *is* current and the repository is already on the latest. The genes are missing from

## 8. Task 6 — what a physiologically faithful representation would need

No code is proposed, as instructed. What follows is the shape of the thing, and the decisions it forces.

**A separate endosomal compartment, and the endosome parted from the lysosome.** `NEURON_MAM01413l`, the endolysosome
store of decision 9.75, lumps the two (0 in-edges, 2 out-edges on v5). The physiology these two genes carry is
specifically **not** lysosomal: the one systematic isoform study found no significant lysosomal localisation (Q1), and
NHE6-null neurons are a case where the endosome and the lysosome move *together* in pH but *oppositely* in function —
both more acidic, yet lysosomal proteolysis down and endosomal cathepsin D activity up (Q12). A single lumped node
cannot hold that, because the two signs would cancel. Minimally the graph needs an **early/recycling endosome**
compartment distinct from the lysosome; Reactome's own compartment set (§6) offers `early endosome lumen` and
`late endosome lumen`, so a two-way split is available from the source, and whether the project needs the recycling
endosome as a third is the question Q18's disagreement with Reactome raises.

**An endosomal pH node, not a pH weight.** The quantity both genes change is luminal pH, and the item already notes
that `linear_response_encoder.py`'s compartment pH weight is inert — `calibrate_ph_sensitivity` is called by no
trainer code (recorded under 9.75). Every downstream arm in §3 runs *through* pH: TrkB, surface GLAST, cathepsin D
activation, endosome–lysosome fusion. So pH here is not a modifier of an edge, it is the mediator, and routing these
genes into an inert weight would, as the item says, change nothing. An endosomal pH node is what the mechanism asks
for; whether the project wants one is an owner ruling the item already names.

**The edges, in the order the sources support them.** The well-sourced core, each with the sign from §3: a leak edge
from the exchanger onto endosomal luminal H+ (negative on H+, i.e. positive on pH) and the matching positive edge onto
luminal Na+/K+; from endosomal pH, a positive edge to TrkB protein and phosphorylation and onward to arborisation,
spine density and AMPAR-mediated transmission; a positive edge to endosome–lysosome fusion and thence to lysosomal
proteolysis; and, as the degenerative arm, a negative edge from lysosomal proteolysis to tau aggregation. A
cation-content edge should carry K+ as well as Na+ (§6). The single-sourced edges — M6PR/retromer, exosome release up,
amyloid-β, glutamate clearance — are listed in §3 as single-sourced and should stay out until a second source exists,
or go in explicitly marked. The glutamate arm has a second complication: it was measured in **astrocytes**, and NHE9
transcript is ~1.2-fold higher in astrocytes than in neurons (Kondapalli et al. 2013, full text), so in a neuronal
graph it is a cell-type mismatch, not just a thin source.

**Three further things a faithful version needs, which are easy to leave out.**
1. **The MAR05430 catalysis has to go, or be justified.** It is not a conservative extra edge: it actively moves
   neuronal cytosolic and extracellular Na+ and extracellular H+ in response to an SLC9A9 perturbation, which is a
   wrong answer rather than a missing one. Keeping it for a numerical reason would need a recorded justification under
   CLAUDE.md "Modelling".
2. **SLC9A6 needs a node from somewhere**, and Reactome R-HSA-425986 is the available route (§6) — at the cost of
   sharing the catalyst entity with SLC9A7, its own membership decision.
3. **NHE6's plasma-membrane transit is real but is not a resident pool** (Q3, Q6). If a surface edge is ever added for
   NHE6 it should be a recycling flux, not a steady-state plasma-membrane exchanger — the exact mistake MAR05430 makes
   for NHE9.
