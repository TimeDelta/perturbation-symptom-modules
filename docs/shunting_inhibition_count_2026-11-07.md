## 1. The list, with quotes

The candidate set is the 35 drugs in `drug_entry_nodes.parquet` whose targets include a `GABR*` or `GLR*` protein
and the 11 GABA_A / glycine-receptor gene perturbations in the v5 evidence reports — 46 perturbations in all.
(`GLRX5` matches the `GLR` prefix in the evidence table but is glutaredoxin-5, not a glycine-receptor subunit, and
is excluded.) Every quotation below is from the PubMed abstract unless the entry says otherwise; what is held is
stated in each BibTeX note in section 5.

### 1.1 The general statement that GABA_A inhibition has two separable parts

Ulrich 2003, *Eur J Neurosci* 18(8):2159–2165, whole-cell recordings from layer V pyramidal cells of rat
somatosensory cortex, with a dynamic-clamp shunt:

> "GABAA receptors mediate inhibition via an increase in membrane conductance (shunting) and/or membrane potential
> hyperpolarization."

and, on what the conductance alone does:

> "Increasing the dynamic clamp shunting conductance led to a parallel shift of the current-discharge curves and a
> reduced slope of the current-voltage relationship, i.e. a decrease of neural gain."

That is item 10's point in the source's own words: the second part changes the slope, which no edge into a scalar
`VM_c` carries. Mitchell & Silver 2003, *Neuron* 38(3):433–445, cerebellar granule cells, states the gain effect
directly: "tonic inhibition reduces the gain and shifts the offset of cerebellar granule cell input-output
relationships" and "Shunting inhibition scales subthreshold voltage".

### 1.2 Group A — perturbations whose main published mechanism IS a conductance-dominated (tonic, extrasynaptic) action

Ten of the 46. For each, the source separates the conductance route from the synaptic one and puts the drug's or
subunit's action on the conductance route.

| Perturbation | id | type | Source and quote |
|---|---|---|---|
| propofol | `CID100004943` | drug | Bieda & MacIver 2004, *J Neurophysiol* 92(3):1658–1667, adult rat hippocampal CA1 slices: "**Gabazine (20 microM) completely blocked both evoked and spontaneous IPSCs but failed to block the propofol-induced depression of intrinsic excitability, implicating tonic, but not phasic, GABA(A) inhibition**" and "suppression of CA1 neuron intrinsic excitability, by these anesthetics, is largely due to activation of tonic GABA(A) conductances" |

### 1.3 Group B — the other 36: not so stated

**Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs (25 of the 46) are explicitly NOT in group A.** Nusser & Mody 2002,
*J Neurophysiol* 87(5):2624–2628, adult rat dentate gyrus granule cells:

> "the benzodiazepine agonist zolpidem (0.5 microM) augmented phasic inhibition by 66%, while leaving the mean tonic
> conductance unchanged"

So the benzodiazepine-site agonists — alprazolam, bromazepam, chlordiazepoxide, clobazam, clonazepam, diazepam,
estazolam, flunitrazepam, flurazepam, lorazepam, midazolam, oxazepam, quazepam, remimazolam, temazepam, triazolam,
zaleplon, zolpidem, zopiclone — act on the phasic synaptic current, which carries both a conductance and a
reversal-potential component (Ulrich 2003), and no source read here calls their main mechanism the shunt.
This is the pass's most load-bearing negative result: the drugs with the most labels in the candidate set are the
ones whose main mechanism is not shunt-dominated.

Also not stated, each for its own reason:

- **Barbiturates** (pentobarbital, secobarbital, methohexital, primidone) and **meprobamate**: no source read in
  this pass states a conductance-dominated action. Bieda & MacIver 2004 covers thiopental only, which is why
  thiopental is in group A and these are not.
- **Enflurane, sevoflurane, desflurane**: Caraiscos et al. 2004 tested isoflurane. Extending it to the other three
  volatiles would be an inference, so they are left out and the gap is named.
- **Topiramate, acamprosate, cenobamate**: GABA_A is one of several published targets for each, and none of the
  sources read makes the shunt its main mechanism.
- **`GABRA1`, `GABRA2`, `GABRA3`, `GABRB2`, `GABRB3`, `GABRG2`**: the synaptic subunits. The counterweight here is
  Pavlov et al. 2009, *J Neurosci* 29(48):15341–15350, rat CA1 pyramidal cells: "**tonic GABA(A) receptor-mediated
  currents in pyramidal cells exclusively affect offset and not gain**", because "tonically active GABA(A)
  receptors in CA1 pyramidal cells show marked outward rectification". So even where a tonic conductance exists,
  the shunt is not always the effect; this bounds how far group A's reasoning can be pushed.
- A second counterweight for the glycine receptor: Milenković & Rübsamen 2011, *Physiol Res* 60(Suppl 1):S15–27:
  "In auditory brainstem neurons of mammals, [Cl(-)]<sub>i</sub> undergoes a developmental shift causing the actions of GABA
  and glycine to gradually change from depolarization to the 'classic' hyperpolarizing-inhibition before hearing
  onset", while in mature birds the gradient is kept so as "to enable depolarizing, but still very efficient,
  shunting inhibition". In the mature mammalian case this source puts the weight on hyperpolarisation, not the
  shunt — the opposite of group A's reading, in a different cell class. The project's own registered constants sit
  between the two: V_rest − E_Cl = −72.6 − (−83.8) = **+11.2 mV** (`docs/membrane_potential_constants.md`, Set A,
  Düsterwald et al. 2018).

**Count: 10 of 46 candidate perturbations have a source stating a conductance-dominated main mechanism; 36 do not,
and for 25 of those 36 a source states the contrary.**

## 3. The routes: how each seed leaves its own nodes

Hop profiles are the relation counts of the edges leaving the frontier at each hop, from the seed nodes the
evidence build names in `perturbation_nodes`. Reach is not reported, for the reason the task gives.

### 3.1 `VM_c` is five hops away and the Cl- pool four

For every one of the 46, including both the gene seeds and the `DRUG:` entry nodes:

```
GENE:<sym>            --encodes-->            PROTEIN:<sym>                  (hop 1)
DRUG:<cid>            --targets-->            PROTEIN:<sym>                  (hop 1)
PROTEIN:<sym>         --binds / member_of-->  protein / protein_entity       (hop 2)
RCTE_975268 etc.      --catalyzed_by-->       RCT_975340 / 975389 / 975449   (hop 3)
RCT_975340 etc.       --changes_ion_pool-->   NEURON_MAM01442c / 01442e      (hop 4)
NEURON_MAM01442c/e    --driving_force-->      VM_c                           (hop 5)
```

The three chloride-transport reactions each carry exactly two `changes_ion_pool` edges (`+1` to the cytosolic pool,
`-1` to the extracellular pool, weight 1) and one `product_of` to hydrochloride; **none of them carries any edge to
`VM_c`**, as 9.112 records. `VM_c`'s only in-edges are the six `driving_force` edges from the Na+, K+ and Cl- pools
(weights 0.318, 0.318, 0.0909 × 4); its only out-edges are nine `voltage_gates` edges to reactions. So the chloride
route is the one route from a GABA_A or glycine seed to `VM_c`, and it is the concentration route, not the
conductance route.

### 3.2 The route that is NOT `VM_c`: `regulates_release`, reached at hop 3

Four curated receptor-complex nodes carry `regulates_release` edges, and they are the shortest mechanistic exit
from the receptor:

- `RCPT_GABRA1`, `RCPT_GABRB2`, `RCPT_GABRG2` (each `member_of` from `PROTEIN:GABRA1`, `PROTEIN:GABRB2`,
  `PROTEIN:GABRG2`) each carry **four** `regulates_release` edges, to `RCT_888589` (release of GABA at the
  synapse), `RCT_210430` (release of L-glutamate at the synapse), `RCT_380901` (release of docked serotonin-loaded
  synaptic vesicle) and `MAR_HISTAMINE_RELEASE` (release of histamine at the synapse).
- `RCPT_GLRA1` (from `PROTEIN:GLRA1`) carries **one**, to `RCT_210430`.

So for a seed touching `GABRA1`, `GABRB2`, `GABRG2` or `GLRA1`, the four transmitter-release reactions sit at
**hop 3** and `VM_c` at hop 5. **37 of the 46 perturbations have this route**; the nine that do not are
bromazepam, cenobamate, `GABRA2`, `GABRA3`, `GABRA5`, `GABRB3`, `GABRD`, `GLRA2` and `GLRB`.

**This is the sharp result for item 10's "labels and the head" option, and it cuts the wrong way.** The
`regulates_release` route exists, is shorter than the `VM_c` route, and is a conductance-independent way for a
receptor perturbation to move downstream — but it is missing for exactly the group A members whose main mechanism
is the shunt: `GABRA5`, `GABRD`, `GLRA2` and `GLRB` have no `RCPT_*` node, so their only mechanistic exit is the
chloride pool and hence `VM_c`. Measured distances (hops from seed), 7 November 2026:

### 3.4 What this says about the item's two non-architectural options

- **The labels and the head.** Partly answered, and not favourably. A conductance-independent route exists
  (`regulates_release`, hop 3) for 37 of the 46, so for those the shunt is a matter of magnitude on an existing
  direction. For the four group A genes `GABRA5`, `GABRD`, `GLRA2` and `GLRB` it does not exist, and their 9 of the
  38 group A pairs (13 of them counting `GLRA1`) rest on the chloride-pool route alone.
- **The input side.** Nothing here forbids it. The six `driving_force` weights are the only inputs to `VM_c`, so a
  per-perturbation scaling of those six would be a one-row change with no new node type; whether that is
  physiological enough to pass CLAUDE.md "Modelling" is a question for 9.112 and is not settled here.

## 4. How large the shunt is beside the hyperpolarisation

Four quantitative sources, mammalian central neurons, measured not modelled except where stated.

**(a) At the reversal potential the shunt is the whole of the inhibition.** `staley1992shunting`, already in
`docs/references.bib` and already cited by 9.112 — Staley & Mody 1992, *J Neurophysiol* 68(1):197–212, adult rat
dentate gyrus granule cells, whole-cell at 34 °C:

> "Measured at its reversal potential, the GABAA receptor-mediated postsynaptic conductance (GGABA-A) decreased the
> peak DPSP amplitude by 35%, shunted 50% of the charge transferred to the soma by the excitatory PSC, and
> completely inhibited the NMDA receptor-mediated component of the DPSP."

The paper also fixes the sign of the direct effect in that preparation: "The GABAA receptor-mediated PSC reversed at
−66 mV, which was 19 mV positive to the resting membrane potential (−85 mV)". So there the direct effect on V_m was
**depolarising** and the inhibition was entirely the shunt — the extreme case of what item 10 describes. The
project's registered constants give a smaller gap in the same direction, +11.2 mV
(`docs/membrane_potential_constants.md`).

**(b) An input-resistance measurement for the glycine receptor.** Maguire et al. 2014,
*Neuropsychopharmacology* 39(5):1232–1244, rodent dorsal raphe serotonergic neurons:

> "for DR neurons strychnine increases their input resistance, induces membrane depolarization, and consequently
> augments their excitability"

Blocking the tonic GlyR conductance raises R_in and depolarises, so in that cell the conductance both shunts and
hyperpolarises; the paper gives the direction of both but no ratio. No percentage change in R_in is in the abstract,
which is all that is held (the PMC full text, PMC3957119, returned an empty body).

**(c) A benzodiazepine does not change the conductance that shunts.** Nusser & Mody 2002,
*J Neurophysiol* 87(5):2624–2628, adult rat dentate gyrus granule cells: "the benzodiazepine agonist zolpidem
(0.5 microM) augmented phasic inhibition by 66%, while leaving the mean tonic conductance unchanged", against
"the mean tonic current is approximately four times larger than that produced by spontaneous synaptic currents
occurring at a frequency of approximately 10 Hz". **So the asked-for quantity — an input-resistance change under a
benzodiazepine — is not what the literature read here reports, because at these concentrations the drug does not
change the standing conductance at all.** It lengthens IPSC decay, which raises the charge of each synaptic event.

**(d) Where the shunt dominates, the conductance route carries the drug's effect and the synaptic route does not.**
Bieda & MacIver 2004: gabazine "completely blocked both evoked and spontaneous IPSCs but failed to block the
propofol-induced depression of intrinsic excitability". Cheng et al. 2006, for etomidate: the tonic conductance rose
"markedly" "whereas synaptic transmission was only slightly enhanced". Caraiscos et al. 2004, for isoflurane:
the tonic conductance responds at 25 µM while synaptic potentiation "occurs only at high concentrations
(>100 microm)" — a greater-than-fourfold separation in concentration.

**(e) The limit on all of this.** Pavlov et al. 2009 shows the shunt is not automatic: in rat CA1 pyramidal cells
the tonic conductance rectifies outwardly, so "tonic GABA(A) receptor-mediated currents have a minimal effect upon
subthreshold membrane potential variation due to synaptic noise" and "exclusively affect offset and not gain".
**No source read in this pass gives a shunting-to-hyperpolarising ratio as a single number.** Staley & Mody 1992 is
the closest: a 50 percent charge shunt with a depolarising direct effect, which is a ratio of the shunt to zero
hyperpolarisation rather than a ratio between two nonzero parts. Reporting a ratio would require either a
dynamic-clamp decomposition at the project's own E_Cl or a pump-leak calculation, neither of which is read here.

## 7. What is NOT settled by this pass

- **No shunting-to-hyperpolarising ratio was found as a number** (section 4e). Two reputable sources were tried for
  it beyond the ones quoted — PubMed searches for a hyperpolarising/shunting decomposition and for an
  input-resistance change under a benzodiazepine — and the second returned the result that the question is
  mis-posed for benzodiazepines, which do not change the standing conductance (Nusser & Mody 2002). A ratio would
  need a dynamic-clamp decomposition at the project's own E_Cl, or a pump-leak derivation from Düsterwald et al.
  2018's Table 1, neither of which is read here.
- **Ganaxolone's own mechanism is unread.** It is in the list on a class inference from Stell et al. 2003, flagged
  in the entry and in section 1.2.
- **Which `GLRA*` subunit carries the dorsal-raphe tonic conductance is unread**, so the three glycine genes are in
  the list together, flagged in the entry.
- **Enflurane, sevoflurane and desflurane are left out** for want of a source; only isoflurane was tested by
  Caraiscos et al. 2004. If the owner wants the volatiles treated as a class, that is a decision, not a reading.
- **Whether any label depends on the shunt is still unmeasured.** Section 2.2 shows no construct is carried only by
  the ten, which is weaker than showing that none depends on them: that would need the encoder, and the v5 graph
  carries the 9.112 sign error, so it cannot be run for this purpose until 9.112 is ruled and rebuilt.
- **Nothing here is a ruling.** Item 10 stays parked; this is its first step, done.
