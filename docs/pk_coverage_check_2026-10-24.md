# PK coverage for the 222 study drugs, and whether QSAR priors are needed (measured 24 October 2026)

A record. Measured on 24 October 2026 against the repository at `a9503af` (the merge of pull request #17 on `main`),
drug list `configs/drug_administration_routes_v4.csv` (222 drugs, build name `v4`). The measurement was written up
in a session note outside the repository and brought here on 25 October 2026; **its scripts were kept in a session
scratchpad, were lost with that container, and the measurement has not been reproduced.** The figures of sections 2
to 4 therefore rest on the note alone. Section 1 quotes figures from repository documents and was checked against
them on 25 October 2026 (the checks are stated where they apply). Nothing here changes a label, a graph or a model
input. The drug table counted against is unchanged on `main` as of 25 October 2026 (222 drugs, no orphenadrine);
orphenadrine joins it at the rebuild owed under decision 1.2 (item 33), after which the counts move by up to one drug.

## The headline

QSAR priors are not needed for half-life, clearance or protein binding. They would be needed for brain penetration,
the one parameter where coverage is thin, and brain penetration is also the parameter QSAR predicts worst. So the
hybrid "measured where available, QSAR otherwise" plan is close to backwards: it adds machinery where the data are
adequate and does not fix the place where they are not.

**97 of the 222 drugs are inside the half-life QSAR's own training set.** For those a "predicted" half-life is
memorisation, not prediction, and the published benchmark accuracy does not apply to them in either direction.

## 4. The four-way split for half-life

| | drugs |
|---|---|
| in Obach's training set **and** a label half-life | 63 |
| in Obach's training set only | 34 |
| label half-life only | 75 |
| **neither** | **50** |

172 of 222 are covered by a tabulated or label value. The 50 remaining are the only candidates for a QSAR prior, and
that 50 is an **upper bound** (see the limits below).

The two sources are complementary in the useful direction. Of the 43 drugs in **no** TDC ADMET set at all, mostly the
newer agents (lumateperone, cariprazine, brexpiprazole, lurasidone, pimavanserin, brivaracetam, cenobamate,
perampanel, daridorexant, lemborexant, suvorexant, atogepant, ubrogepant, lasmiditan, pitolisant, istradefylline,
opicapone, vilazodone, tasimelteon), **27 have a numeric half-life in their label**. The QSAR training sets are
old-drug biased; the labels cover what they miss.

## 5. Limits of this measurement, which are not small

- **The scripts are lost and the measurement is not reproduced.** Sections 2 to 4 are the note's figures, taken on
  trust from a measurement no one can rerun as it was run. Section 7 says how to rebuild it.
- **The 50-drug gap is an upper bound, not a count.** openFDA's `openfda.generic_name` field is unpopulated for many
  SPLs, so the name query misses labels that exist. Alfentanil is the clear case: `openfda.generic_name:alfentanil`
  returns no match while a free-text search returns 1,744 label records. A second pass over the 29 drugs with no
  structured-name match recovered only 4, so the miss is systematic rather than a tail. Drugs with plainly existing US
  labels still in the gap list (alfentanil, maprotiline, secobarbital, rotigotine, esketamine, ganaxolone,
  ubrogepant) are name-resolution failures, not absent data.
- **Pattern matching is not value extraction.** "half-life with a number attached" means the text states one, not
  that a usable value was parsed, disambiguated between parent and metabolite, or tied to a route and population. The
  repository's existing label work (`configs/drug_label_protein_binding.csv`) records a sentence, a set_id and an
  effective_time per value; that is the standard this count does not meet.
- **The Obach overlap is a proxy for one model's training set**, not for all published half-life QSARs.
- **Skeleton matching on the first InChIKey block** merges stereoisomers and salt forms. That is deliberate (the drug
  is the same chemical entity) but it over-counts overlap where a dataset holds one enantiomer and the study drug is
  the racemate.
- A further 25 drugs appear to have no US label at all (agomelatine, bromazepam, dothiepin, moclobemide, reboxetine,
  sulpiride, zopiclone and others). Under the rule that a failed source is not a finished search (`CLAUDE.md`,
  "Reading sources"), these need a second agency (EMA, Health Canada or the UK eMC) before absence is recorded.

## 6. What this implies for the proposed PK layer

1. **Do not build QSAR priors for half-life.** 172 of 222 are already covered, the true figure is higher than that,
   and 97 of the "predictions" would be lookups of the model's own training rows.
2. **Extract label PK instead.** It is public domain through openFDA, it covers the newer drugs the intravenous
   compilations miss, and the repository already has the extraction machinery and the provenance convention. The
   work is name resolution and value parsing, not modelling.
3. **The binding constraint is Kp,uu,brain: 94 of 222, human for 13.** Every unbound brain concentration in the
   current table rests on a rodent ratio assumed to hold at the therapeutic concentration. A peak-exposure layer
   built on top of that inherits the assumption and adds a time course to a quantity whose steady-state value is
   already mostly extrapolated from another species.
4. QSAR for brain penetration is credible only as a binary gate. Quantitative Kp,uu models reach roughly 2-fold at
   best, against a parameter spanning orders of magnitude, so a predicted value would not distinguish the drugs the
   model needs to distinguish (`docs/brain_exposure_data_feasibility.md`, route 3).

## 7. Reproducing this

The scripts were short: resolve the drug list to InChIKeys through PubChem PUG REST (the identifier format in
`configs/drug_administration_routes_v4.csv` is `CID1` plus a zero-padded eight-digit PubChem CID, not `CID` plus the
CID); download the TDC `admet_group` archive from Harvard Dataverse; match on the first InChIKey block with RDKit;
query openFDA `drug/label` per drug name. If the measurement is worth keeping, the rebuilt script belongs in
`experiments/` beside `build_drug_exposure_table.py`, and its output replaces sections 2 to 4 here as a dated addition.
PubChem's `/rest/` path is disallowed for automated readers by its robots file (`docs/label_source_review.md` section
35), so a rebuild resolves identifiers through another route or through the identifiers the drug table already holds.
